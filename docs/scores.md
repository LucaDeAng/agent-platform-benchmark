# Scores: three dimensions and a synthetic index

Generated by `scripts/benchmark.py` from `data/evidence.csv`, `data/criteria.csv` and `data/dimensions.csv`. Snapshot of public pages accessed 2026-11-08. 17 platforms, 24 criteria, 3 dimensions. Rules: [METHOD.md](../METHOD.md), section "Dimension scores and synthetic index", and changes 8–10. Background: [evaluating-agents.md](evaluating-agents.md).

**These scores measure what vendors publish, not what their products do.** A high lower bound means that many capabilities are documented in public pages. A wide range means that little is documented, often because the vendor keeps its documentation behind a login or because our search for that platform was narrower. Nothing here was tested hands-on. Evidence was collected and re-checked by LLM agents under the author's supervision (see the README).

## How to read a score

- Every score is a **range** between 0 and 1. These are worst-case and best-case bounds (partial identification): without an assumption about why a cell has no documentation, they are the narrowest statement the evidence supports.
- Values per cell: documented = 1 at both bounds; vendor states (marketing, blog or press only) = 0.5 for the lower bound and 1 for the upper bound; not found and not applicable = 0 for the lower bound and 1 for the upper bound. Publishing more never lowers either bound.
- A dimension score is the mean of its criteria; the index is the mean of the three dimensions (equal weights). **Coverage** is the share of cells with any evidence (documented or vendor states). The width of a range is the share of cells not found, plus half the share of vendor-states cells.
- No midpoint is published: it would amount to guessing a value for every unknown cell.
- **Every upper bound is 1.** Nothing in public documentation rules a capability out, so the best case is always full marks. The informative numbers are the **lower bound** (the floor set by published evidence) and the **coverage**. A lower bound is not a ranking: any platform could reach 1 if its gaps were documented.
- No rank is published, only how wide a platform's rank interval is. Rows are in alphabetical order.

## Does a ranking hold?

The rule was written in METHOD.md before any score was computed. A ranked list is published only if (a) fewer than half of all platform pairs have overlapping index ranges, and (b) every platform's rank moves by at most 2 positions across the sensitivity scenarios, at both bounds.

- (a) 136 of 136 platform pairs (100%) have overlapping index ranges: **not met**.
- (b) 14 of 17 platforms have a rank interval wider than 2 places at one bound or both: **not met**.

**A league table would be misleading, so none is shown.**

**Bands.** METHOD.md groups platforms by interval dominance (platform B dominates A when B's lower bound is above A's upper bound). With the default value table, no platform dominates another: all 17 platforms fall into a single band. **The public evidence does not separate them.**

Under the value table used before change 8 (vendor states = 0.5 at both bounds, which let a published claim lower the upper bound), the same data gave 3 bands: Decagon (band 3), ElevenAgents (ElevenLabs) (band 2), Parloa (band 3), Salesforce Agentforce (band 2), Sierra (band 3) sat below band 1. 3 of those 5 platforms keep their product docs fully or partly behind a login. That separation was an artefact of the convention, not of the evidence.

Width of the index range: from 0.04 to 0.64. Median width 0.28 for the 11 platforms with `partial` or `none` search coverage, 0.16 for the 6 with `full` coverage; 0.57 for the 3 platforms with gated product docs (Decagon, Parloa, Sierra).

## Ways to misread these scores

- **Documentation is not capability.** The index counts what is published. A vendor with gated docs has many vendor-states or not-found cells, which widens its range and says nothing about the product.
- **The lower bound is not "the score".** It assumes every unknown is absent and every claim is worth half; the upper bound assumes both are fully documented. Neither is an estimate, and the range is not a confidence interval.
- **Uneven search.** Platforms with `partial` or `none` search coverage (METHOD.md, change 2) have not-found cells that rest on a narrower search, so their lower bounds may be too low.
- **The value table was changed after the first results** (METHOD.md, change 8). The earlier table is kept as a sensitivity scenario so that the effect can be checked.
- **Presence, not depth.** A criterion scores the same whether the feature is basic or extensive, generally available or in preview, voice-only or omnichannel. Recorded values are not scored.
- **Suites are scored as a whole.** For Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Salesforce and Genesys, evidence can come from several products, including QA products that may cover human agents only.
- **Salesforce owns two scored platforms** (Salesforce Agentforce and Fin). They are scored separately.
- **Compensation.** The index is a weighted mean: a high score in one dimension offsets a low one in another. Read the dimension scores and the gap table, not only the index.
- **Rank-interval widths are not ranks.** A width of 0 would mean a fixed position, not a top position.
- **Commercial transparency is in the governance dimension.** `pricing_model` and `deploy_model` score whether a billing unit and a deployment model are published, not whether they are favourable.
- **Snapshot.** Pages change. The access date of every cell is in `data/evidence.csv`.
