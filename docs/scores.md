# Scores: three dimensions and a synthetic index

Generated by `scripts/benchmark.py` from `data/evidence.csv`, `data/criteria.csv` and `data/dimensions.csv`. Snapshot of public pages accessed 2026-11-08. 17 platforms, 24 criteria, 3 dimensions. Rules: [METHOD.md](../METHOD.md), section "Dimension scores and synthetic index". Background: [evaluating-agents.md](evaluating-agents.md).

**These scores measure what vendors publish, not what their products do.** A high score means that many capabilities are documented in public pages. A low lower bound often means that the vendor keeps its documentation behind a login, or that our search for that platform was narrower. Nothing here was tested hands-on. Evidence was collected and re-checked by LLM agents under the author's supervision (see the README).

## How to read a score

- Every score is a **range** between 0 and 1. The **lower bound** counts every cell without evidence as 0 (capability absent). The **upper bound** counts it as 1 (capability documented). As far as public documentation goes, the truth lies somewhere in between.
- **Coverage** is the share of cells with evidence (documented or vendor states). The width of a range is exactly 1 − coverage: a wide range means that little is known, not that the product is uneven.
- Values per cell: documented = 1; vendor states (marketing, blog or press pages only) = 0.5; not found and not applicable = 0 for the lower bound and 1 for the upper bound. A dimension score is the mean of its criteria; the index is the mean of the three dimensions (equal weights).
- No midpoint is published: it would amount to guessing 0.5 for every unknown cell.
- Rows are in alphabetical order. "Does a ranking hold?" below explains why there is no league table.

## Does a ranking hold?

The rule was written in METHOD.md before any score was computed. A ranked list is published only if (a) fewer than half of all platform pairs have overlapping index ranges, and (b) every platform's rank moves by at most 2 positions across the sensitivity scenarios, at both bounds.

- (a) 98 of 136 platform pairs (72%) have overlapping index ranges: **not met**.
- (b) 14 of 17 platforms have a rank range wider than 3 positions at one bound or both: **not met**.

**A league table would be misleading, so none is shown.** Platforms are grouped into bands instead, by interval dominance on the default index: platform B dominates platform A when B's lower bound is above A's upper bound. Band 1 holds the platforms that no platform dominates; band 2 those dominated only by band-1 platforms; and so on. Within a band, platforms are listed alphabetically and are not ordered.

A band says which platforms the public evidence can separate. It is not a quality grade: a platform can be in band 1 because its range is wide (much is unknown), not because much is documented.

## Ways to misread these scores

- **Documentation is not capability.** The index counts what is published. A vendor with gated docs (Sierra, Decagon, Parloa) has many vendor-states or not-found cells. That lowers its lower bound and widens its range, and says nothing about the product.
- **The lower bound is not "the score".** It assumes every unknown is absent; the upper bound assumes every unknown is documented. Neither is an estimate, and the range is not a confidence interval.
- **Uneven search.** Platforms with `partial` or `none` search coverage (METHOD.md, change 2) have not-found cells that rest on a narrower search, so their lower bounds may be too low.
- **Vendor states counts half, and the bands depend on it.** A marketing claim scores 0.5 by convention. If claims counted as documentation (`vendor_states` = 1), the platforms would fall into 1 band(s) instead of 3, and the largest upper-bound rank shift would be 16 places. Any separation of the vendors with gated docs from the rest depends on this convention.
- **Presence, not depth.** A criterion scores the same whether the feature is basic or extensive, generally available or in preview, voice-only or omnichannel. Recorded values are not scored.
- **Suites are scored as a whole.** For Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Salesforce and Genesys, evidence can come from several products, including QA products that may cover human agents only.
- **Salesforce owns two scored platforms** (Salesforce Agentforce and Fin). They are scored separately.
- **Compensation.** The index is a weighted mean: a high score in one dimension offsets a low one in another. Read the dimension scores, not only the index.
- **Bands are not grades.** A band groups platforms that the evidence cannot separate; a wide range can put a platform in band 1.
- **Commercial transparency is in the governance dimension.** `pricing_model` and `deploy_model` score whether a billing unit and a deployment model are published, not whether they are favourable.
- **Snapshot.** Pages change. The access date of every cell is in `data/evidence.csv`.
